About Onuri

A different model for notes.

Onuri: Notes & Life Tracker is an iPhone notes app built around a simple idea: human memory is associative. A thought can belong to a person, a place, a date, a time and a topic at once. Onuri connects those signals as you write so useful structure can emerge without asking you to maintain it first.

The idea behind Onuri

Computers file. People remember by association.

Onuri grew from a second-brain idea about the difference between how computers traditionally organise information and how human memory works.

Computers are built around files, folders and fixed hierarchies. Human memory is less rigid. A single memory can be connected to who was there, where it happened, when it happened and what it was about — all at the same time.

Onuri was designed around that model. Instead of asking you to decide where every note belongs, it looks for meaningful context inside the writing itself. The aim is simple: write first, let the connections emerge, then find the right thing again later.

What Onuri connects

Context that already exists in your writing.

Onuri recognises useful signals as you write and turns them into ways to filter, follow and rediscover your notes.

People

Who a note is about

Reconnect entries around the people who appear in your writing and the context you have recorded around them.

Places

Where things happened

Follow places across your notes and rediscover writing connected to locations you have mentioned.

Dates & times

When things connect

Use dates and times as part of the context around an entry rather than relying only on when the note itself was created.

Topics

What keeps coming up

Follow recurring subjects through your writing without building a manual taxonomy before you start.

Related meaning

Connections beyond exact words

Related entries can surface writing with similar meaning even when two notes do not use the same vocabulary.

Insights

Patterns over time

Onuri Premium adds deeper views of recurring themes, momentum, people and place patterns, semantic clusters and strongest connections.

Who Onuri is for

For people who want useful notes without maintaining a system.

Write without deciding where it belongs

Capture an idea, conversation, project thought or everyday memory before worrying about folders, notebooks or filing rules.

Remember people and context

Keep the surrounding details of conversations, relationships and encounters in the same writing you already create.

Build a personal knowledge base naturally

Let accumulated notes become more connected over time instead of manually linking every entry.

Rediscover old writing

Search text, filter by connected context and follow related entries when you remember part of something but not exactly where you put it.

Where Onuri fits

Part of the modern note-taking and PKM landscape.

Onuri sits in the broader note-taking and personal knowledge management category alongside established products such as Apple Notes, Bear, Obsidian, Notion and Evernote.

Those products take different approaches to notebooks, folders, tags, databases and manual links. Onuri's distinction is its emphasis on automatic contextual connections: write naturally first, then use the people, places, dates, times, topics and semantic relationships already present in the writing.

It is not intended to reproduce every workflow of those apps. Its focus is a lighter-maintenance way to capture personal information and make it easier to recover later.
