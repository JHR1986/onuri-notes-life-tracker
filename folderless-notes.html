Why folders get in the way

Folders are useful when a note has one obvious home, but real thoughts often overlap across projects, people and timelines. A folderless approach gives you another way to organise notes when one permanent category does not fit.

The folderless workflow

  • Drop and Write: Open Onuri, type your thoughts, and close the app. No categorization dialogs.
  • Associative Retrieval: Let the app discover threads between thoughts based on actual names, dates, and concepts inside your text.
  • Private iCloud Sync: Your unformatted text files sync safely across your Apple devices with zero ads and zero data harvesting.
  • Full Ownership: Export your notes as plain TXT or CSV whenever you want.

Why some notes do not fit one folder

A note about a conversation can also be about a project, a city, a decision and a future date. Folderless note-taking avoids forcing that entry into one permanent category. Onuri keeps the original writing together and recognises several kinds of context inside it.

Organisation still exists — it is just lighter

Folderless does not mean unsearchable or random. You can search ordinary text, return through recognised people, places, dates and topics, and export your notes when you want a copy outside the app. The structure comes from what you wrote rather than from a directory tree you have to maintain.

Who is a folderless notes app for?

This approach suits people who abandon elaborate organisation systems, capture a mixture of personal and practical notes, or remember context more easily than folder names. If carefully curated folders already work well for you, there is no need to replace them; Onuri is an alternative for people who prefer capture-first organisation.
