A life tracker for people who do not want to maintain a tracking system

Many life tracking apps begin with a dashboard: habits to tick, moods to score, goals to quantify, fields to complete and categories to maintain. That can be useful when measurement is the goal. But much of life is harder to reduce to a checkbox. A conversation, a decision, an idea, a place you discovered or something you want to remember often needs a sentence, not a statistic.

Onuri takes a different approach. It works as a personal life log, daily notes app, memory tracker and automatic second brain through the same simple action: writing. Capture what happened in your own words and let useful structure emerge afterwards.

Use daily notes as a searchable personal life log

A daily life tracker does not have to mean logging every hour. A few lines can preserve the context that disappears surprisingly quickly: who you met, where you went, what you decided, what you learned, what you promised to do and what was on your mind.

Because entries are dated and searchable, Onuri can act as a lightweight life logging app, personal timeline, daily record, work log, idea log or memory log. You can write frequently or only when something feels worth keeping.

Remember people, conversations and relationship context

Names are part of the information Onuri can recognise and connect automatically. Mention a friend, colleague, client or family member naturally in a note and later revisit the entries connected with that person. This makes Onuri useful when you want a personal relationship notes app, conversation log, people notes system or lightweight personal CRM without turning relationships into a database.

It is not about tracking another person's location or activity. It is about remembering your own conversations, plans and shared context.

Connect places, trips and everyday memories

Place names can become another route back into your writing. Notes about a neighbourhood, city, restaurant, office or trip can gather naturally around the locations you mention. Over time, this creates a searchable record of places visited, travel notes, trip memories and location-based life moments without asking you to build a travel database.

Track ideas, decisions, projects and recurring themes

Life tracking is also about what you were thinking. Onuri can connect recurring topics across notes, helping you revisit an idea you nearly forgot, the reasoning behind a decision, a project that keeps resurfacing or a theme that has appeared repeatedly in your writing.

That makes the same app useful as an idea tracker, decision log, project notes app, thought journal, personal knowledge base and second brain. Instead of deciding where every thought belongs before you write it, you can capture first and rediscover through search and connections later.

Find old notes even when you only remember part of the story

The practical value of a personal life archive is being able to find something again. Onuri combines fast full-text search with connected people, places, dates and topics. If you remember a name but not the date, a place but not the wording, or a topic but not which note contained it, those connections give you additional paths back.

This is the core of Onuri's automatic second brain approach and its folderless note-taking workflow: your archive becomes more navigable without requiring you to maintain an elaborate filing system.

A private life tracker for personal notes

A record of your life can contain sensitive context, so privacy matters. Onuri is designed around on-device processing, no advertising trackers and no third-party analytics. Notes remain on your device and can sync through your personal iCloud account. You can also export your writing as TXT or CSV so your personal archive is not trapped inside the app.

If you are looking for a private life tracker, private diary alternative, local-first notes app or personal journal without ad tracking, this architecture is deliberately different from services built around collecting behavioural data.

One writing habit, many ways to track a life

The same natural-note workflow can cover many everyday needs: a daily life log, notes about friends and family, work conversations, meeting follow-ups, travel memories, places to revisit, books and ideas, decisions, personal projects, goals, gratitude, lessons learned and moments you simply do not want to forget.

Onuri is intentionally not a specialist medical tracker, GPS tracker, finance tracker or streak-based habit tracker. Its strength is the unstructured context around your life — the information that is meaningful precisely because it does not fit neatly into a metric.

Questions people ask about tracking everyday life

What is a life tracker app?

A life tracker app helps you keep a useful record of everyday life. Onuri approaches life tracking through natural notes: write about people, places, dates, ideas, projects and moments, and the app automatically connects recurring context so you can find it again later.

How can I track my life without spreadsheets or habit charts?

Use Onuri as a lightweight life log. Instead of filling in fields or maintaining categories, write a normal sentence about what happened, what you decided, who you met or what you want to remember. Onuri builds connections from the writing itself.

Can I use Onuri as a daily life log or personal timeline?

Yes. Dated entries naturally create a record of your days, while connected people, places and topics let you revisit related moments across time.

Can I keep notes about people and relationships?

Yes. When names recur in your writing, Onuri can connect the notes in which those people appear. This makes it useful for remembering conversations, shared plans and personal context without maintaining a separate contact database.

Can I track places I visit and things that happened there?

Yes. Onuri recognises places in natural writing and can connect entries that share the same location, helping you rediscover trips, restaurants, meetings and other place-based memories.

Is Onuri a habit tracker or health tracker?

Onuri is not a specialist medical, fitness or streak-based habit tracker. It is a flexible notes and life tracking app for capturing the context of everyday life in your own words.

Is my life tracking data private?

Onuri is designed around local-first processing with no advertising trackers or third-party analytics. Notes stay on your device and can sync through your personal iCloud account.

Can Onuri replace a journal or diary app?

For people who prefer short notes, observations and searchable life context, it can. Onuri is especially suited to writing that you want to reconnect later by person, place, date or topic rather than by manually organised journal folders.

How does Onuri help me remember things about my life?

It combines full-text search with automatic connections. You can search what you remember or follow a recognised person, place, date or topic back to related entries.

Is Onuri free to use?

Onuri includes unlimited note creation, automatic connections, iCloud sync and data export in the free experience, with optional Premium insights for deeper patterns and themes.
