What Is Personal Knowledge Management?

Personal knowledge management (PKM) is the practice of capturing, organising, retrieving and reusing information that matters to you. It can include ideas, research, project context, conversations, decisions, places, plans and observations accumulated over time.

The challenge is not usually capturing information. It is making old information useful again. Systems that depend on perfect folders, taxonomies, backlinks or tagging can work well for disciplined users, but they also create maintenance. When the organising habit breaks, retrieval becomes harder.

Onuri takes an automatic approach: write the note first, then let recognised people, places, dates and topics provide multiple paths back to it later.

Connected Notes Without Manual Knowledge-Base Maintenance

A personal knowledge base becomes more valuable when one note can lead naturally to another. Onuri identifies recurring context inside ordinary prose and uses it to connect related writing. A person can connect a meeting note to a travel memory. A place can connect an idea to an older event. A recurring topic can reveal a thread you never deliberately categorised.

This is different from forcing every thought into one hierarchy. Notes can stay independent and still participate in a wider network of context. That makes the system more forgiving when your interests change or when a note belongs to several areas of life at once.

The same principle is explored in more depth on the folderless note-taking page.

Search, Connections and Rediscovery

Good PKM is not just storage. It is retrieval at the moment old context becomes useful. Onuri combines full-text search with connection-based navigation so you can approach your notes from more than one direction.

If you remember the wording, search can take you directly to the note. If you only remember a person, place, date or related topic, connected context can give you another route. This reduces dependence on remembering exactly where you filed something months ago.

As your note history grows, rediscovery becomes a core feature rather than an afterthought. The system can surface relationships across notes that were written at different times for different reasons.

Automatic PKM as a Second Brain

The phrase second brain describes an external system that helps capture and retrieve information you do not want to hold entirely in memory. Onuri’s approach is automatic: useful structure emerges from the writing itself instead of requiring a large setup phase.

You can use the same system for project ideas, life tracking, reading notes, conversations, decisions or personal reflections. The common layer is context—who, where, when and what the note relates to. For a focused explanation, see Automatic Second Brain for iPhone.

A Private Personal Knowledge Base You Can Export

Long-term knowledge systems deserve long-term ownership. Onuri processes note content on device, supports private iCloud sync, and does not use advertising trackers or third-party analytics SDKs inside the app.

Your writing can be exported as TXT or CSV files, giving you a portable representation of the material you have accumulated. That matters more as a knowledge base becomes valuable: the more useful the history, the more important it is that you retain control of it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a personal knowledge management app?

A personal knowledge management app helps you capture, organise, retrieve and reuse information that matters to you. Onuri does this through natural notes, automatic recognition of useful context, connected notes and search.

Is Onuri a PKM app or a notes app?

It is a notes app that can function as a personal knowledge management system. You write ordinary notes, while people, places, dates and topics can become connections that make the accumulated writing easier to rediscover.

Do I need to manually create backlinks or tags?

No. Onuri is designed to recognise useful entities and create contextual connections without requiring manual wiki links, folder trees or a tagging system.

How does Onuri help with note rediscovery?

Onuri combines full-text search with recognised people, places, dates and topics. This gives you several ways to return to older notes even when you do not remember their exact wording or location.

Can I export my personal knowledge base?

Yes. Onuri supports TXT and CSV export so your accumulated writing can remain portable and accessible outside the app.
