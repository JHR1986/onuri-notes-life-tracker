Where it started

The whole system is one note.

Most reading logs fail for the same reason most journals do: they ask for too much. A spreadsheet wants columns filled in. A book app wants a rating, a shelf, a start date and a review. After a few months the log falls behind, and once it falls behind it stops being trusted.

This case study started with a single line typed into Onuri after a book club meeting:

That is the entire entry. Four pieces of information, separated by dashes, written in about ten seconds. No title field, no folder called “Books”, no tags typed by hand. Onuri recognises the useful parts of the note on your iPhone and shows them as pills, so the month, the book, the author and the group all become things you can search and filter by later.

Repeat that once per book and, by the end of the year, you have something more useful than most reading apps give you: a private reading history you can question in plain words. What did we read in August? Which Brontë books have I read? What did Dad recommend? What did I read on holiday?

Mock-ups on this page show the pattern in Onuri’s style. Exactly which pills appear depends on what Onuri recognises in your own wording.

01

Search by month

“What did we read in August?”

Type August into search and the answer comes straight back: Jane Eyre, Charlotte Brontë, book club.

This is the question book clubs ask constantly, usually while scrolling back through a group chat. Because the month is written in the note itself, it stays attached to the book no matter when you typed it. The same search works for any month of the year, and if you have been logging for several years, each note also carries the date you wrote it, so you can tell this August from last August at a glance.

Author searches are where a one-line log starts to feel like a library catalogue. The same works for Austen (Pride and Prejudice in January, Persuasion in July) or any writer you keep coming back to. One practical tip: type names the same way every time. If you sometimes write Brontë and sometimes Bronte, search for the part they share, such as Bront.

04

Search by person

“What did Dad recommend?”

Tap the Dad pill and Dracula comes back, alongside everything else you’ve written about him.

Recommendations are the most forgotten part of reading. Someone tells you about a book, you read it months later, and by then you can’t remember who suggested it, so you can’t tell them you finally read it. Writing their name in the note fixes that. Because Onuri connects people across all your notes, the same tap also shows Dad’s birthday note and the gift idea you saved, which is often exactly when a book recommendation is useful.

Search by place

Keeping track of months

Why the month goes in the note itself.

Every Onuri note already has the date you wrote it. So why type the month too? Because the day you write a note and the month you read the book are often different.

  • You can log your backlog in one sitting. Sit down in October, type out everything you read since January, and every book still lands in the right month.
  • Book club months are fixed. “The August book” stays the August book even if you finished it on 2 September.
  • It reads naturally. When you scroll back, “August - Jane Eyre” tells you more at a glance than a timestamp does.
  • It makes seasonal patterns visible. Over a few years you start to notice your own rhythms: heavier classics in winter, lighter books in summer, or the months you barely read at all.

If you read more than one book a month, that’s fine. Write a note for each. If you want the year too, add it: August 2026 - Jane Eyre - Charlotte Brontë - Book club. Searching “August” still finds it, and searching “2026” gives you that year’s full list.

Copy the pattern

The reading log template.

Start with the one-line version. Add a second line only when there is something worth keeping.

The one-liner

Month - Title - Author - Context

Context is whoever or whatever you’ll want to search for later: Book club, a person’s name, a place, or why you picked it up.

With a thought

August - Jane Eyre - Charlotte Brontë - Book club
Better than I expected. Aoife hated the ending.

One sentence of reaction is enough to remember what you thought, and the second name connects the note to Aoife too.

With a quote

August - Jane Eyre - Charlotte Brontë - Book club
Quote: "I am no bird; and no net ensnares me"

Months later you might remember the line but not the book. Search a word from it, like bird, and the whole note comes back with the title and author attached.

To read next

To read - Rebecca - Daphne du Maurier - Sarah recommended

Start with “To read” for your list of future books. Search “To read” for the full list, and change it to the month once you’ve read it.

For book club organisers

Running a book club from your notes.

If you’re the one who keeps the group organised, the same habit covers the logistics too. Notes like these sit right beside the reading log:

  • Whose turn is it to host? Filter by Book club and look at the names.
  • Who picked what? Write the picker’s name in the note: “Sarah’s pick”.
  • Which books split the group? One line of reaction after each meeting builds a history you can look back on at the end of the year.
  • What’s been suggested but not chosen yet? Keep “Book club idea - Title - Author - Who suggested it” notes and search “Book club idea” when it’s time to vote.

Questions

Reading log FAQ

What is the easiest way to keep track of books I’ve read?

Write one short line per book as soon as you finish it, in the same order every time: month, title, author, and one word of context such as who recommended it or which group you read it with. Keeping it to one line is what makes the habit last.

What should a reading log include?

At minimum: when you read it, the title and the author. The most useful extra is context, meaning who recommended it, who you discussed it with or where you read it, because that’s the detail people most often wish they had kept. A single sentence of reaction or one favourite quote is a good optional second line.

How can I remember which month I read a book?

Write the month at the start of the note, for example “August - Jane Eyre - Charlotte Brontë - Book club”. Searching the month later brings back everything you read then, even if you logged the book weeks afterwards.

Can I use Onuri as a book club tracker?

Yes. Add “Book club” to each note about the group’s picks, and filter by it to see everything the club has read. Notes about hosts, dates and times, such as “Book club at Aoife’s on Thursday at 7pm”, connect to the same group.

Can I add books I read years ago?

Yes. Write the month and year in the note, for example “March 2023 - Middlemarch - George Eliot - Book club”. The month and year in the text keep it searchable by when you read it, not when you typed it.

Is Onuri a replacement for Goodreads or The StoryGraph?

Not exactly. Book apps are better for covers, ratings, reading challenges and stats. Onuri is better for private, fast notes that connect each book to the people, places and months in the rest of your life. Many readers use both.

Is my reading log private?

Yes. Onuri has no public profiles or social features. Notes stay on your device and in your own iCloud account, and the app’s App Store privacy label is “Data Not Collected”.

Is Onuri free?

Yes. Onuri is free to download with unlimited notes, search and automatic connections. Optional Onuri Premium adds Insights, Ongoing and Done statuses, colour themes and alternate app icons.
